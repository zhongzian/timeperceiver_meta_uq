## Score reasons

### Journal of Forecasting

- Scope (25/25): The official journal identity directly centers theoretical, practical, computational, and methodological forecasting.
- Novelty (14/20): The empirical heavy-tail design study fits forecasting methodology, but fixed Student-t is not a new distribution or theoretical contribution.
- Experiments (12/15): Proper scores, fixed masks, repeated primary-backbone seeds, a second backbone, and negative ablations are persuasive; breadth remains three datasets.
- Forecast fit (10/10): Every main outcome concerns forecast accuracy, probabilistic scores, interval behavior, or robustness to missing histories.
- Format (9/10): Free Format and no located length cap make the existing 18-page source readily adaptable, with only presentation streamlining expected.
- Negative/repro (5/5): The learnable-df, FiLM, conformal, and point-tolerance findings provide useful diagnostic evidence for forecasting methodology.
- Policy (5/5): Free Format, single-blind review, LaTeX support, supporting information, and data-sharing expectations are compatible with current assets.
- Risk-adjusted (7/10): Excellent topical alignment lowers desk risk, while moderate novelty, compact data breadth, and statistical-depth expectations keep overall risk moderate.

### Neural Computing and Applications

- Scope (24/25): The official scope explicitly names intelligent forecasting, benchmarks, practical neural computing, and performance measures.
- Novelty (14/20): A controlled neural probabilistic-head contribution is plausible, but moderate novelty needs careful differentiation from a routine layer swap.
- Experiments (13/15): The fixed-mask audit, four primary-backbone seeds, second backbone, ablations, and negative findings suit benchmark-oriented evidence.
- Forecast fit (9/10): Forecasting is explicitly named and recent neural forecasting papers confirm active readership.
- Format (8/10): The double-blind workflow matches the current anonymous artifact, while 30 visual/table assets create moderate layout work.
- Negative/repro (4/5): Benchmark and performance-measure scope rewards rigorous audits, although negative-result reporting is not an explicit editorial focus.
- Policy (5/5): Anonymization, data declarations, supplementary files, and AI disclosure are all compatible with existing project assets.
- Risk-adjusted (7/10): Strong documented scope fit lowers desk risk, with moderate novelty and compact dataset breadth remaining the main review risks.

### Applied Intelligence

- Scope (21/25): Probabilistic neural forecasting and incomplete information fit, but forecasting is not the central scope and method novelty is part of scope screening.
- Novelty (13/20): The controlled fixed-head study is defensible, while the official emphasis on innovative methodology makes moderate novelty a material risk.
- Experiments (12/15): Two backbones, multiple TimePerceiver seeds, ablations, and fixed masks are solid; three datasets and one PatchTST seed limit breadth.
- Forecast fit (8/10): Recent articles confirm probabilistic-forecasting readership, although the journal serves a broader applied-AI community.
- Format (8/10): No official length cap was found and Springer LaTeX is supported; the figure and table count still creates adaptation work.
- Negative/repro (5/5): The fixed-mask correction, preregistered tolerance, and retained negative findings materially strengthen an empirical systems paper.
- Policy (4/5): Data, code, funding, conflict, and AI declarations are manageable; review anonymity remains unverified.
- Risk-adjusted (5/10): Documented scope overlap is offset by a high novelty-mismatch risk compared with recent method-heavy papers.

### Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence

- Scope (11/25): AI forecasting and missing sensor data are relevant, but the official real-world engineering-application requirement is not met by the current generic benchmark paper.
- Novelty (12/20): The fixed probabilistic head has practical value, while moderate AI novelty is weak against the journal's explicit novel-AI and engineering framing.
- Experiments (10/15): The fixed-mask evidence is rigorous but lacks application-specific engineering datasets, decisions, and deployment analysis.
- Forecast fit (7/10): Forecasting is represented in recent content when linked to engineering domains such as electric load.
- Format (6/10): Single-column and double-anonymized formatting are feasible, but the title, abstract, and narrative require substantial non-template reframing.
- Negative/repro (3/5): Rigorous negative results help, but they cannot compensate for the missing engineering-application contribution.
- Policy (5/5): The project can satisfy anonymization, editable-source, supplementary, conflict, funding, and AI-disclosure requirements.
- Risk-adjusted (2/10): The unresolved real-world engineering scope blocker creates a very high desk-rejection risk.
